# A Bookkeeping Category Is a Ledger, Not an Income Stream

A category in Monarch (or any bookkeeping tool) collects **everything filed
against a thing**. It is not the income that thing produces. Never wire a
category straight to an income card and label it with the income's name.

Define the income narrowly and positively — money IN, from the known
counterparty, of the expected kind — and exclude everything unrecognised
rather than netting it.

## Why

`303 Hartstene Dr` fed the "Rent" card directly. The category also carried
HOA/ClickPay, county tax, Home Depot, plumbing, security deposits in and out,
an Uber, a Great Wolf Lodge Zelle, an $11 Zelle from a stranger, and a $6,000
bank reversal that had nothing to do with the property. August 2026 read
$7,864 against a $6,400 lease; no month had ever matched the lease.

The failure is quiet. A netted card always shows *a* number, so it looks
healthy while being wrong in both directions at once — property costs
suppressing good months, stray credits inflating bad ones.

## How to apply

- Identify the stream by counterparty + direction + kind (rent = money in,
  from a tenant on the tenant list, memo not "deposit"), not by category
  membership.
- Exclude what you cannot positively identify. Overstating an expense or
  understating an income is the safe direction; an unexplained credit
  quietly cancelling real costs is not.
- Keep the tenant/employer/counterparty list explicit and commented, so a
  change of tenant fails loudly instead of silently zeroing the stream.
- Costs that fall out of the income line still exist. Decide where they go —
  do not let them vanish from every view. See
  [[attribute-income-to-its-stated-period]] for the timing half of this.
